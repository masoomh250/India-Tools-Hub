What Is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular monthly payment made by a borrower to repay a loan over a specified period.

An EMI generally contains two components: the principal amount and the interest charged by the lender. At the beginning of many reducing-balance loans, a larger portion of the EMI goes toward interest. As the outstanding principal decreases, the interest component generally reduces.

How Is EMI Calculated?

The standard reducing-balance EMI formula uses three important inputs:

  1. Principal: The amount borrowed.
  2. Interest rate: The annual rate charged on the loan.
  3. Tenure: The total loan period.
Standard EMI Formula
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]

P = Principal loan amount
r = Monthly interest rate
n = Total number of monthly instalments

Converting the annual interest rate into a monthly rate

If the lender quotes an annual interest rate, the monthly rate used in the formula is generally calculated by dividing the annual rate by 12.

Monthly Rate = Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12

For example, an annual interest rate of 8.5% becomes approximately 0.7083% per month, expressed as a decimal as approximately 0.007083.

What Factors Affect Your EMI?

1. Loan Amount

A larger loan generally results in a higher EMI when the interest rate and tenure remain unchanged.

2. Interest Rate

A higher interest rate generally increases the EMI and the total interest paid over the loan tenure.

3. Loan Tenure

A longer tenure can reduce the monthly EMI, but you may pay more total interest because the loan remains outstanding for a longer period.

4. Prepayments

Part-prepayments can reduce the outstanding principal. Depending on the lender and loan terms, this may help reduce the overall interest burden or shorten the repayment period.

Longer Tenure vs Shorter Tenure

Choosing a loan tenure involves a trade-off between monthly affordability and total interest.

Tenure Typical Effect on EMI Typical Effect on Total Interest
Shorter Higher monthly EMI Lower total interest
Longer Lower monthly EMI Higher total interest

How Much of Your EMI Goes Toward Interest?

For a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal. Because the outstanding principal usually decreases as you repay the loan, the interest component generally decreases over time.

This means the composition of your EMI can change throughout the loan tenure even when the scheduled EMI remains the same.

EMI vs Total Interest

Looking only at the monthly EMI can sometimes give an incomplete picture of the cost of borrowing.

For example, a loan with a lower EMI may appear more affordable, but if it has a much longer tenure, the total interest paid can be substantially higher.

Before taking a loan, it is useful to compare:

  1. Monthly EMI
  2. Total interest payable
  3. Total repayment amount
  4. Loan tenure
  5. Interest rate

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular monthly payment made toward a loan and generally includes both principal and interest.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI is calculated using the principal amount, monthly interest rate and total number of monthly instalments. The standard formula is P × r × (1+r)n ÷ [(1+r)n−1].

Does a longer loan tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. A longer tenure can reduce the monthly EMI, but it usually increases the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Can I calculate home loan EMI online?

Yes. An online EMI calculator can estimate your monthly EMI, total interest and total repayment based on the loan amount, interest rate and tenure.

Is the EMI shown by a calculator the exact amount charged by a bank?

An EMI calculator provides an estimate based on the values entered. The actual repayment schedule can differ depending on the lender's terms, interest calculation method, fees and other conditions.

Final Takeaway

EMI depends mainly on the loan amount, interest rate and repayment tenure. While increasing the tenure can make the monthly payment more manageable, it can also increase the total interest paid.

Therefore, when comparing loans, don't look only at the EMI. Consider the interest rate, total interest, total repayment and loan tenure together.
