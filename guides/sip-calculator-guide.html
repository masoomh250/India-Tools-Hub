What Is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly every month.

A SIP is an investment method rather than a separate investment product. Actual performance depends on the underlying investment and market conditions.

How Are SIP Returns Calculated?

A SIP calculator estimates the future value of a series of periodic investments. The main inputs are monthly investment, expected annual return and investment period.

FV = P × [((1 + r)ⁿ − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)

P = periodic investment amount
r = periodic rate of return
n = number of investment periods

SIP Example: ₹10,000 per Month for 10 Years

Monthly investment: ₹10,000

Expected annual return: 12%

Investment period: 10 years

Estimated maturity value: approximately ₹23.23 lakh

Total amount invested: ₹12 lakh
Estimated gain: approximately ₹11.23 lakh

What Is the Power of Compounding?

Compounding means returns can generate additional returns over time. With regular investing, the effect can become increasingly important over longer periods.

The actual result depends on the returns achieved by the underlying investment.

What Factors Affect SIP Returns?

Monthly investment: A larger contribution generally increases the potential corpus.

Expected return: A higher assumption produces a higher estimated value but is not guaranteed.

Investment duration: More time can provide more opportunity for compounding.

Contribution timing: The timing of periodic contributions affects the mathematical result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan and is a method of making regular investments.

How are SIP returns calculated?

The estimate uses the periodic investment amount, assumed return and investment duration.

Does SIP guarantee returns?

No. SIP does not guarantee a particular investment return.

Can I increase my SIP over time?

Yes. Increasing periodic contributions over time is commonly called a step-up SIP.
