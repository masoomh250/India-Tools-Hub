What Is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly every month.

SIPs are commonly associated with mutual funds. Instead of investing a large amount at one time, an investor contributes smaller amounts regularly according to a chosen schedule.

A SIP is an investment method, not a separate investment product. The actual investment performance depends on the underlying investment and market conditions.

How Are SIP Returns Calculated?

A SIP calculator estimates the future value of a series of periodic investments. The main inputs are:

  1. Monthly investment: The amount invested every month.
  2. Expected annual return: The assumed yearly rate of return.
  3. Investment period: The number of years the SIP continues.

A commonly used monthly SIP future value formula is:

Illustrative SIP Future Value Formula
FV = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)

P = Periodic investment amount
r = Periodic rate of return
n = Number of investment periods

The final multiplication by (1 + r) represents an assumption that each monthly contribution is invested at the beginning of the monthly period. Different calculators can use different timing conventions, so results may vary slightly.

Converting annual return into a monthly rate

For a simple monthly estimate, the annual expected return is divided by 12 to obtain the monthly rate used in the calculation.

Monthly Rate = Annual Expected Return ÷ 12

What Is the Power of Compounding?

Compounding means that returns can generate additional returns over time. With regular investing, the combination of periodic contributions and compounding can become increasingly significant as the investment period becomes longer.

This is one reason why the investment period is an important input in any SIP calculation.

Why time matters

Increasing the investment period gives the invested money more time to potentially grow. However, the actual result depends on the returns achieved by the investment.

What Factors Affect SIP Returns?

1. Monthly Investment

A larger monthly investment generally results in a larger potential corpus when the assumed return and investment period remain unchanged.

2. Expected Rate of Return

A higher assumed rate produces a higher estimated maturity value in a calculator. However, the assumed rate should not be interpreted as a guaranteed future return.

3. Investment Duration

A longer investment period provides more time for contributions and potential compounding to work.

4. Investment Frequency

The timing and frequency of contributions affect the mathematical calculation. Monthly SIP calculators generally assume a fixed monthly contribution schedule.

SIP vs One-Time Investment

SIP and lump-sum investing are different ways of investing.

Feature SIP Lump Sum
Investment pattern Regular contributions One-time investment
Cash requirement Spread over time Larger amount initially
Calculation Multiple periodic investments Single investment
Market exposure Investments occur over multiple dates Amount is invested at one point in time

What Is a Step-Up SIP?

A step-up SIP increases the periodic investment amount over time. For example, an investor may start with ₹10,000 per month and increase the contribution by a fixed amount or percentage each year.

Increasing contributions can help investors align their investment strategy with increasing income, although the affordability of the higher contribution should always be considered.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly every month.

How are SIP returns calculated?

A SIP calculator estimates the future value of periodic investments using the investment amount, assumed rate of return and investment duration.

Does SIP guarantee returns?

No. SIP does not guarantee a particular return. If the underlying investment is market-linked, its actual value can rise or fall.

Can I increase my SIP investment over time?

Yes. Investors can increase their periodic investment over time. This approach is commonly called a step-up SIP.

Is the SIP calculator result guaranteed?

No. A SIP calculator provides an estimate based on the assumptions entered. Actual investment returns may be different.

Final Takeaway

SIP calculations depend mainly on the periodic investment amount, expected return, investment duration and timing of contributions. Compounding can become increasingly important over longer periods.

However, a calculator's projected maturity value is only an estimate. Investment returns can vary, particularly when the underlying investment is market-linked.
