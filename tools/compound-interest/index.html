What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest accumulated during previous compounding periods.

Unlike simple interest, compound interest allows previously earned interest to generate additional interest over time.

Compound Interest Formula

The standard compound interest formula is:

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt

Where:

A = Final amount
P = Initial principal
r = Annual interest rate expressed as a decimal
n = Number of compounding periods per year
t = Investment period in years

Compound interest earned can then be calculated as:

Compound Interest = A − P

Example of Compound Interest

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 at an annual interest rate of 8% for 10 years with monthly compounding.

The calculator applies the compound interest formula using 12 compounding periods per year and estimates the final value.

Because interest is added to the balance during each compounding period, the investment can grow faster than it would under simple interest at the same nominal rate.

How Compounding Frequency Affects Returns

The same annual interest rate can produce different results depending on how frequently interest is compounded.

Yearly compounding

Interest is compounded once per year.

Half-yearly compounding

Interest is compounded twice per year.

Quarterly compounding

Interest is compounded four times per year.

Monthly compounding

Interest is compounded twelve times per year.

Daily compounding

Interest is mathematically compounded 365 times per year in this calculator.

Compound Interest vs Simple Interest

With simple interest, interest is calculated only on the original principal. With compound interest, previously accumulated interest also participates in future growth.

For long investment periods, this difference can become significant. The actual result depends on the interest rate, time period and compounding frequency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between principal and final amount?

Principal is the amount you initially invest. The final amount is the principal plus the compound interest earned during the investment period.

Does more frequent compounding always increase the final amount?

For the same nominal annual rate and investment period, more frequent compounding generally produces a higher mathematical final amount, assuming all other conditions remain the same.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

If the interest rate is zero, no interest is earned and the final amount remains equal to the principal.

Can I use this calculator for investments?

Yes. You can use it to understand compound growth mathematically. However, actual investment returns can differ from a fixed compound interest calculation because many investments have variable returns, fees, taxes and other conditions.
