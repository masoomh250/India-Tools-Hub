What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the amount a borrower generally pays to a lender each month towards a loan.

An EMI normally consists of two components: the principal amount borrowed and the interest charged by the lender. During the early stages of many amortising loans, a larger portion of the EMI goes towards interest. As the outstanding principal reduces, the interest component generally decreases.

An EMI calculator helps you estimate how much you may need to pay every month before comparing different loan amounts, interest rates and repayment periods.

How is EMI calculated?

The standard reducing-balance EMI formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where:

P = principal loan amount
r = monthly interest rate in decimal form
n = total number of monthly instalments

For example, an annual interest rate of 8.5% is converted to a monthly rate by dividing 8.5% by 12 and then converting the percentage into a decimal.

EMI calculation example

Suppose you borrow ₹10,00,000 at an annual interest rate of 8.5% for 20 years.

Using the standard reducing-balance EMI formula, the estimated monthly EMI is approximately ₹8,678.

The estimated total payment is approximately ₹20.83 lakh, including approximately ₹10.83 lakh of interest over the full 20-year tenure.

The actual amount charged by a lender can differ because of rounding, processing fees, insurance, taxes, rate changes or other loan-specific charges.

What affects your loan EMI?

1. Loan amount

A larger loan amount generally results in a higher monthly EMI if the interest rate and tenure remain unchanged.

2. Interest rate

A higher interest rate generally increases both the monthly EMI and the total interest paid over the loan tenure.

3. Loan tenure

Increasing the tenure can reduce the monthly EMI, but it usually increases the total interest paid because the loan remains outstanding for a longer period.

Longer vs shorter loan tenure

A shorter tenure generally means higher monthly payments but lower total interest. A longer tenure can make the monthly EMI easier to manage, but the overall interest cost can be substantially higher.

For example, extending a loan from 15 years to 20 years may reduce the monthly EMI, but the borrower may pay considerably more interest over the complete repayment period.

Before choosing a loan tenure, compare both the monthly EMI and the total amount payable rather than looking only at the EMI.

Reducing balance EMI vs flat interest

This calculator uses the standard reducing-balance EMI method. Under this method, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, which generally decreases as loan payments are made.

A flat interest calculation works differently because interest may be calculated using the original principal for the agreed period. Therefore, a quoted flat interest rate should not be directly compared with a reducing-balance interest rate without understanding the lender's calculation method.

Can prepayment reduce loan interest?

Making an eligible loan prepayment can reduce the outstanding principal and may reduce the interest payable over the remaining tenure.

The actual benefit depends on the lender's terms, prepayment rules, interest rate, remaining tenure and whether the borrower chooses to reduce the EMI or the loan tenure.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EMI calculator free?

Yes. The India Tools Hub EMI Calculator is free to use and does not require registration.

Can I use this calculator for a home loan?

Yes. You can use it to estimate EMI for home loans, personal loans, vehicle loans, car loans and other loans that use a standard reducing-balance repayment structure.

Can I use this calculator for a personal loan?

Yes. Enter the personal loan amount, annual interest rate and repayment tenure to estimate the monthly EMI and total interest.

Does a longer tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. Increasing the loan tenure spreads the repayment over more months, which usually reduces the monthly EMI. However, the total interest paid is generally higher.

Does the calculator include processing fees?

No. The calculator estimates EMI, interest and total repayment based on the loan amount, interest rate and tenure entered. Processing fees, insurance, taxes and other lender-specific charges are not included.

Does the calculator support zero interest?

Yes. If the annual interest rate is entered as 0%, the calculator divides the loan amount by the total number of monthly instalments.

Are my loan details stored?

The calculation is performed in your browser. The tool does not require you to create an account to calculate your EMI.

Is this calculator suitable for actual loan decisions?

The calculator provides an estimate for informational purposes. Before taking a loan, verify the final EMI, interest rate, fees, charges and repayment schedule with your lender.
