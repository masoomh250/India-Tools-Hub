How to use the FD Calculator

Enter your deposit amount, annual interest rate and investment period, then select Calculate maturity. The calculator instantly estimates the maturity amount and interest earned.

How is FD maturity calculated?

This calculator uses the standard compound interest formula with annual compounding:

A = P × (1 + r)n

Where:

P = Principal deposit amount
r = Annual interest rate expressed as a decimal
n = Investment period in years
A = Estimated maturity amount

FD calculation example

Suppose you deposit ₹2,00,000 at an annual interest rate of 7% for 3 years.

Using annual compounding, the estimated maturity amount is approximately ₹2,45,010.86.

The estimated interest earned is approximately ₹45,010.86.

Actual bank FD maturity may differ because banks may calculate interest using different compounding frequencies and deposit terms.

What affects FD returns?

1. Deposit amount

A higher deposit amount generally results in higher interest earnings when the interest rate and tenure remain unchanged.

2. Interest rate

A higher FD interest rate generally increases the maturity amount and total interest earned.

3. Investment tenure

A longer investment period generally allows more time for interest to accumulate. The actual benefit depends on the interest rate and compounding method.

4. Compounding frequency

The frequency at which interest is compounded can affect the final maturity amount. This calculator uses annual compounding for simplicity.

FD vs savings account

A fixed deposit generally provides a predetermined interest rate for a specified period, while a savings account usually offers greater liquidity but may provide a different interest rate.

Before investing, compare the interest rate, tenure, premature withdrawal rules, tax treatment and other terms offered by your bank.
