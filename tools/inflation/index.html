See how inflation can affect the future price of today's expenses, savings goals and purchases.

This calculator uses a constant annual inflation assumption. Actual inflation can change from year to year.

What is Inflation?

Inflation is the general increase in prices of goods and services over time. As prices rise, the purchasing power of the same amount of money generally decreases.

For example, if an item costs ₹1,00,000 today and prices increase by 6% every year, the same item could cost significantly more after several years.

Inflation Calculator Formula

The estimated future cost is calculated using compound inflation:

Future Cost = Current Cost × (1 + Inflation Rate)Years

The annual inflation rate should be entered as a percentage. The calculator converts it into a decimal before performing the calculation.

Example

Suppose something costs ₹1,00,000 today and inflation averages 6% per year for 10 years.

The estimated future cost would be approximately:

₹1,00,000 × (1.06)10 = ₹1,79,085.85

This means the estimated additional cost caused by inflation would be approximately ₹79,085.85.

How Inflation Affects Purchasing Power

Inflation does not only increase future prices. It also reduces the amount of goods and services that a fixed amount of money can purchase over time.

For example, if inflation averages 6% annually, ₹1,00,000 today would have approximately the purchasing power of ₹55,839 after 10 years, assuming the same constant inflation rate.

The purchasing power calculation used by this calculator is:

Future Purchasing Power = Current Amount ÷ (1 + Inflation Rate)Years

Why Inflation Matters for Financial Planning

Inflation can have a significant impact on long term financial goals. A retirement corpus, education fund, house purchase or other future expense may require a larger amount than today's cost suggests.

Using an inflation assumption can help estimate how much a future expense might cost and how much money may be required to meet that goal.

Important Limitations

This calculator assumes the same inflation rate for every year. Real inflation does not necessarily remain constant.

Different goods and services can also experience different rates of price increase. Therefore, the result should be treated as an estimate rather than a prediction of an actual future price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an inflation calculator show?

It estimates what a current amount could cost in the future if prices increase at a specified annual inflation rate.

How does inflation reduce purchasing power?

When prices increase, the same amount of money can generally purchase fewer goods and services. The effect becomes more significant over longer periods.

Does this calculator predict actual inflation?

No. It applies the annual inflation rate you enter as a constant assumption. Actual inflation can vary from year to year.

What happens if inflation is 0%?

If inflation is 0%, the estimated future cost remains equal to the current cost.

Can I use this calculator for retirement planning?

Yes. It can help estimate how today's expenses might change in the future under a chosen inflation assumption. Retirement planning also requires considering investment returns, taxes, expenses and other financial factors.
