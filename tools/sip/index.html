What is a SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly every month, into a mutual fund.

Instead of investing a large amount at one time, an investor can invest smaller amounts regularly. Over a long period, the investment can potentially benefit from compounding and disciplined investing.

How does a SIP work?

With a monthly SIP, you invest a predetermined amount at regular intervals. The number of mutual fund units purchased can vary because the market price of the fund changes over time.

When the investment period is longer, the returns can potentially compound for a longer period. However, mutual fund returns are market-linked and are not guaranteed.

How is SIP maturity value calculated?

For this calculator, the estimated SIP maturity value is calculated using the following formula:

FV = M × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)

Where:

M = monthly investment
r = monthly expected rate of return
n = total number of monthly investments

The annual expected return is converted into a monthly rate by dividing it by 12.

This calculator applies the additional (1 + r) factor, which represents SIP contributions being made at the beginning of each month.

SIP calculation example

Suppose you invest ₹10,000 per month for 10 years and assume an annual return of 12%.

You would invest a total of ₹12,00,000 over the 10-year period. Using the calculation convention used by this calculator, the estimated maturity value is approximately ₹23.23 lakh.

The estimated wealth gain would therefore be approximately ₹11.23 lakh.

This is only an illustration. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and actual results can differ significantly from the assumed return.

What affects SIP returns?

1. Monthly investment amount

Increasing the monthly investment generally increases the total amount invested and can increase the potential maturity value, assuming the same return and investment period.

2. Expected rate of return

A higher assumed return increases the estimated maturity value. However, a higher expected return also generally means greater investment risk. Actual mutual fund returns are not guaranteed.

3. Investment period

A longer investment period gives your investments more time to potentially benefit from compounding. Starting early can therefore make a significant difference to the long-term value of an investment.

Why is compounding important for SIP investing?

Compounding means that returns generated by an investment can themselves generate additional returns over time. The effect can become more significant as the investment period increases.

This is one reason long-term investing can potentially produce a substantially higher maturity value than simply adding up the monthly contributions.

SIP vs one-time investment

A SIP involves investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, while a one-time investment involves investing a larger amount at once.

A SIP can help investors maintain a disciplined investment habit and spread purchases across different market levels. However, neither method guarantees a particular return.
